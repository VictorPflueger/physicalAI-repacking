# Repack Pilot — design system

How Repack Pilot looks, why it looks that way, and how to change it without breaking it. Version 0.5.0 (the look moved to Vici), 28 October 2026.

Repack Pilot is built on **Vici**, Victor Pflüger's design system (release v1.0.0, copied unchanged into `vici/`; `vici/README.md` says which files and from where). Vici decides the look: its tokens in a light and a dark theme, its fonts, components, icons and hexagon shapes. This prototype's rules decide the meaning: what a colour stands for, what needs emphasis, what the room must see. Where the two meet, this file says how, and §11 lists every deliberate deviation from Vici with its reason.

Repack Pilot shares its shell with its sister prototypes **Rebate Hunter** (7.2.0), the **Navigator** and the **Timetable Translator**: the same Vici copy, the same shell code and styles, the same colour grammar and agentic patterns. The page icon, the names and the domain words differ, and so does the centrepiece: where the Navigator shows a map and Rebate Hunter a rebate board, Repack Pilot shows the **robot's world model** in its Customer view (§7c): one item's way from the camera through the model to the robot and the scale, the moment, and tonight's pallet as the model holds it, with what it is worth beside it. Its Full view shows the **cell board**: the inbound pallet, the simulated robot cell with its camera, scale and gripper, the manual station and twelve store totes against their truck cut-offs (§7).

---

## 1. The brief

- **On Vici.** The prototype looks like the rest of Victor's work: merlot ink and pastel surfaces, Bricolage Grotesque, Inter and JetBrains Mono, hexagons where other systems draw circles. Its page icon is the card it has on Victor's website (§4).
- **Legible in 60 seconds.** A partner who has never seen the tool should get the point from across a room, on a 1080p projector, often in a lit room: *the robot handles most of the pallet, people handle the rest, these stores make their trucks, and this is what every 1,000 units cost in labour.*
- **Five minutes for a customer** (M4, `TECH_NOTES.md` D50). A head of DC operations sees in one call what it does, how it works and what it is worth: the How it works window opens the call with the engine's figures, the console shows only what matters unless Details is on, every word is plain, and the night ends on what the cell is worth for the customer's own site. `DEMO_SCRIPT.md` is the five-minute script.
- **The world model at the centre** (Victor, 19 October 2026; `TECH_NOTES.md` D62–D69). The call is about what the robot knows and what that is worth to the customer: the Customer view shows the world model at work on scenario 3's night (it knows every item, camera and scale check it, it learns a weight, it hands what it does not know to a person) and prices only labour; what the model catches is counted. The Full view keeps the cell board, the options and the dial one click away.
- **Agentic and physical.** An AI agent plans and a simulated cell acts on screen. At every moment the viewer must be able to tell who is acting (the agent, the cell or the person who has to decide) and what is simulated.

## 2. Methods, and what each one decided

| Method | Source | What it decided |
|---|---|---|
| A design system with semantic tokens | Vici v1.0.0 (`vici/`) | Every colour is one of Vici's semantic tokens (`--vc-text`, `--vc-accent`, `--vc-danger-solid` …), which switch with the theme; no colour values of the app's own (§4) |
| The hexagon rule | Vici, *Hexagon* guideline: "If it would be round, make it a hexagon" | Pips, badges, markers, spinners, the stations' icon cells, tags and pills are flat-top hexagons and hex capsules (§4); `tests/hexagon.mjs` keeps it so |
| Colour-blind separation | Machado, Oliveira & Fernandes (2009) | The night's state colours are measured under simulated colour blindness (`tests/tokens.mjs`); where Vici's tones come close, form carries the difference (§3, §5) |
| Accessibility | WCAG 2.2 | Contrast for every pairing in both themes, the focus ring, target size, no meaning by colour alone |
| Agentic UX patterns | Yocco, *Designing For Agentic AI*, Smashing Magazine, Feb 2026 | The progress rail (intent preview), the autonomy dial, the handover to the person, the audit trail |
| AI labelling | IBM Carbon for AI; Pickering, *Tooltips & Toggletips* | An **AI** label (Vici's feature tag, the sparkle and "AI") on the options, drafts and summary the agent wrote, with a short explanation one click away (`js/ai_label.js`) |
| Emphasis | Google, *Expressive Design: Google's UX Research* (Material 3 Expressive) | One emphasised action per moment: colour, size and containment go to the step that needs the person |
| Projection | VUMC Edge for Scholars, *Optimizing Colors for Projected Presentations* | Pastels never carry meaning alone; every pastel fill has a deeper stroke or text step. The decision view is reviewed under a simulated washout |
| Dashboard patterns | Bach et al., *Dashboard Design Patterns* (2022) | Each KPI tile pairs one value with a line of context; the KPI board keeps Plan (engine) and Tonight (simulation) in labelled columns |

## 3. Colour grammar

Each colour means one thing, everywhere.

| Colour (Vici's tokens) | Means | Where you see it | Never used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Merlot** (`--vc-accent` and its tokens) | **The person in charge** | Primary buttons; every Approve and Confirm entry; the progress step that waits for you (*Your decision*, *Your approval*, *Your confirmation*); the recommended option's frame (2 px of `--vc-accent` on `--vc-accent-soft`), which the confirmation card and, on the Customer view, an item handed to a person and its moment wear too; the person's decisions in the console and the audit; a changed input; the focus ring; the LIVE badge | A mark among state colours: merlot sits close to Vici's danger tone (§5), so a person shows in words, the person's icon and the frame, never as one more coloured stripe |
| **Danger, warning, success** (`--vc-danger-*`, `--vc-warning-*`, `--vc-success-*`) | **The night's states**: a store order late (or a grasp failed), at risk, on time (or a pick landed) | The store lanes and their cut-offs, the tile of a finished item, KPI tints, status tags, the option cards' first line, the cost bars | Decoration, and buttons other than the two destructive ones |
| **Fjord** (`--vc-info-*`) | Readings and checks, and the money an option spends | Camera and scale readings, the grasp rehearsal, a correction written back, *Check* steps in the console, Diagnostics while a test runs, Tonight's column on the KPI board, the extra-labour bar | |
| **Lilac** (`--vc-feature-*`) | **The AI's voice** | AI tags (`vc-tag--feature` with the sparkle and "AI"), hypotheses and revisions, the agent's what-if, the reasoning summaries, the agent's wake-ups, the streaming cursor, model calls in the audit, the AI disclosure | Anything a person decided |
| **Neutral solid** (`--vc-neutral-solid`) | **Goods at rest** | The units in a tote, an item the robot handles, the world model's own frame; the strong border's grey (`--vc-border-strong`) for units by hand | |
| **Ink** (`--vc-text`, Vici's neutrals) | Everything else: the agent's work | Text, tool calls, separators, neutral controls, Reject, guardrail notes, the chosen night speed and autonomy level, the **Simulated cell** tag, **NIGHT PREVIEW**; where a person would otherwise be one more coloured mark among the states, the person's icon in the cell board's legend and the stripe of a person's line in what the model learned, each beside its words | |
| **The tape** (`--vc-text` and `--vc-surface` stripes) | **Scripted replay** | The SIMULATION badge, the console's tape and watermark, the summary's "SIMULATION" prefix when downloaded, always with the word SIMULATION. Outside every state colour (Vici's honey now means *at risk*), so a replay can never pass for a live run | The cell itself, and a live run |

On stage, in one sentence: *"Merlot is you, lilac is the AI, red, honey and green are the store orders against their trucks."*

**Two honesty marks, two different jobs.** The cell is simulated in both modes, so its tag is neutral: Vici's tag on the panel's surface, its capsule outlined in the strong border's grey, with the gripper (`.sim-cell-tag`), in the board's and the stage's top right corner whatever the mode. The SIMULATION tape is reserved for scripted replays, as in the reference prototypes (build brief, "Honesty rules"). Generated data carries a **Synthetic** tag: neutral, in muted ink, its capsule drawn as an outline with dashed long sides and whole pointed ends (`.tag-synthetic`), so it never looks like real data and never like a state. A live run carries the **LIVE** badge. A scenario without a scripted replay plays in Simulation without the agent, and its badge reads **NIGHT PREVIEW** in ink with an ink outline: it is neither a scripted replay nor the live agent (`TECH_NOTES.md` D17, D39).

**Every state has a form of its own** (WCAG 1.4.1), because Vici's state tones are close in lightness and some come close under colour blindness (§5): the words in each lane (*✓ truck 02:40*, *at risk · 2 min to spare*, *late · truck left with 120/300*), the words and icons of the tags and the console's marks (a truck, a tote, the person, the scale, the sparkle), the option cards' first line in words with Vici's state icon (success *Every truck on time in all 20 nights*, warning and danger *late in 9 of 20* and *late in 20 of 20*), a person's frame, and Vici's state icons in Diagnostics. The danger tone appears on two buttons only, **Abort** and **Forget key**, as an outline, because they stop or delete something.

## 4. Where the look comes from

**Colours.** Every colour is one of Vici's semantic tokens, which switch with the theme. The app has no colour values of its own; the favicon is the one exception, because a favicon cannot read CSS. Vici's family primitives (`--vc-teal-100` and so on) appear only where Vici uses them itself (the page icon's teal cell). JavaScript draws no colour: the cell board and the world model take their colours from classes in `css/cell.css`, `css/world.css` and `css/stage.css`, so a theme change repaints them. The roles of the former palette map onto Vici like this:

| Former role | Vici's token |
|---|---|
| `--paper` | `--vc-surface` |
| `--porcelain`, `--porcelain-2` | `--vc-bg`; `--vc-bg-subtle` for tracks, `--vc-surface-hover` for hover |
| `--line`, `--line-strong`, `--border-control` | `--vc-border-subtle`, `--vc-border`, `--vc-border-strong` |
| `--text`, `--text-muted`, `--text-faint` | `--vc-text`, `--vc-text-muted`, `--vc-text-subtle` |
| `--on-brand`, `--brand`, `--brand-deep` | `--vc-on-accent`; `--vc-accent`, `--vc-accent-hover`, `--vc-accent-pressed` |
| `--brand-text`, `--brand-mid`, `--brand-line` | `--vc-accent-text` (merlot text and icons, never among state colours); `--vc-accent-soft-hover` for quiet lines |
| `--brand-soft`, `--brand-wash` | `--vc-accent-soft` (`--vc-surface-selected` for a selected item in a list or menu), `--vc-surface-hover` |
| danger, warning, success, info (fill, mid, line, text) | `--vc-danger-bg`, `-border`, `-solid`, `-text` and the same for success and info; warning uses `--vc-warning-text` for lines too (its solid is a light fill, too pale for a stroke) |
| violet | lilac, Vici's feature tone: `--vc-feature-bg`, `-border`, `-solid`, `-text` |
| `--late`, `--at-risk`, `--on-time` and their fills | the line and mid tokens of danger, warning and success |
| `--goods` | `--vc-neutral-solid` |
| `--live` | `--vc-accent` |
| the hazard yellow and black of the simulation | the neutral tape: `--vc-text` and `--vc-surface`, with `--vc-bg-subtle` as its wash |
| `--canvas` (the board's and the stage's background) | `--vc-bg-sunken` |
| `--r-sm`, `--r-md`, `--r-lg` | `--vc-radius-md`, `--vc-radius-lg`, `--vc-radius-xl` |
| `--shadow-pop`, `--shadow-soft` | `--vc-shadow-lg`, `--vc-shadow-md` |
| `--focus` | Vici's focus ring, `var(--vc-stroke-focus) solid var(--vc-focus)`, hexagonal on hexagon controls |

What it is worth compares labour by hand and with the cell in Vici's data colours 8 and 2 (`--vc-data-8`, `--vc-data-2`): a comparison of two ways of working, not a state and not a person's decision. The app keeps a handful of tokens of its own in `css/style.css`: the type sizes Vici has no step for (`--fs-xs`, `--fs-lg`, `--fs-xl`, `--fs-kpi`; `--fs-sm` and `--fs-md` became Vici's `--vc-font-size-sm` and `--vc-font-size-md`), because the layout and the projector checks are tuned to them, and `--hit`, 44 px, the smallest target for what the room clicks.

**Themes.** Light is the default: the prototypes are mostly shown on projectors in lit rooms. `js/theme.js` sets `data-theme="dark"` only if the visitor chose dark with the header's toggle (Vici's toggle, moon and sun, tooltip *Dark theme*; it follows the AI disclosure on the badges' second row, where Rebate Hunter has it before the disclosure, because this header's first row also holds the view switch and a toggle there would change rows as the KPI tiles change width), `data-theme="light"` otherwise; Vici remembers the choice under `vc-theme`, the key all of Victor's pages share. Every contrast check runs in both themes (§5).

**Type.** Bricolage Grotesque (`--vc-font-display`) only for short display text: the app's name, the KPI figures, dialog and panel titles, the board's and the stage's captions, the figures of What it is worth. Inter (`--vc-font-text`) for all other text, with tabular figures wherever numbers line up (the night clock, the lanes' cut-offs, the KPI board). JetBrains Mono (`--vc-font-mono`) for ids, the console's times, tool names, source keys and raw output. Sentence case for headings, buttons, tabs and labels, except names, codes and the mode markings SIMULATION, LIVE and NIGHT PREVIEW (§11). Reading text runs at most 75 characters per line: paragraphs, notes, list items, the stage's lede and a dialog's note take Vici's prose measure (`--vc-container-prose`, 50ch, about 65 characters of Inter), as the console's entries, the dialogs' paragraphs and the drafts do in the shared shell (`TECH_NOTES.md` D81). Two kinds of text follow their box instead: the stage's moment, a callout built like Vici's alert, which sets no measure either (its lines run to about 135 characters on a 1080p projector; held to the measure it would take two more lines and cost the stage its full size at 23:10), and rows of data: the audit's and Diagnostics' tables and the one-line logs of what the model learned. The root size follows the projector, in relative units so it still follows the reader's browser setting: `clamp(93.75%, 0.48vw + 50%, 112.5%)`, 15 px at 1280 px wide and about 17 px at 1920 px.

**Shapes.** Anything round is a flat-top hexagon, anything pill-shaped a hex capsule with pointed ends (Vici's `--vc-hex`, `vc-hex`, `vc-capsule`, `vc-cell`): status and live pips, count badges, the status, mode, Simulated cell, Synthetic and AI-disclosure tags, the byline's portrait, spinners and pulses, the intro's numbered steps, the rail's markers, the option letters, the menu's scenario numbers, the stations' icon cells on the cell board and the stage. The lanes', the tiles' and What it is worth's bars have square ends. Rectangles keep small radii, up to `--vc-radius-xl`. Other brands' logos stay as their owners publish them (LinkedIn's in the byline). In forced colours (Windows contrast themes) the app's own hexagons keep a visible outline, as Vici's cells do.

**Icons.** Vici's icons (`<span class="vc-icon vc-icon--NAME">`), through `js/icons.js`, which maps the app's names to Vici's (`reset` → `refresh`, `gear` → `settings`, `x` → `close`, `alert` → `warning`, `spark` → `sparkle` …). Thirteen things have no Vici icon yet; the app draws them in Vici's style in the same module (24 × 24 with 2 px to spare, 1.75 px strokes with round caps and joins, hexagons where a shape would be round, chamfered corners) and proposes them for Vici (§12): seven of Rebate Hunter's drawings, exactly, the truck the Navigator and Repack Pilot share, and five of the cell's own. They are written the way Vici writes its own icons, as masks (black strokes on the grid, which only say where the icon is), so they too take the colour of the text around them. Icon-only buttons have Vici's tooltip with their name.

**The page icon.** The prototype's mark is the card it has on Victor's website (data/prototypes.js of VictorPflueger/VIP: icon `layers`, kind *showcase*): Vici's layers icon in a teal hexagon cell, teal because Repack Pilot is a tech-showcase (lilac is for prototypes), in the header (`vc-cell vc-cell--teal`), on the loading screen (`vc-cell--xl`) and, drawn larger and bolder so it reads at 16 px, as `favicon.svg`. If the website's card changes, the page icon changes with it, and the other way round (`tests/page-icon.mjs`).

**The byline.** The drawer bar carries Vici's byline: *Built by*, Victor's hexagon portrait and name, LinkedIn's own logo and a globe for his website, each a 44 px link that opens in a new tab. The app links to its author, never to its own source code.

## 5. The checks

`node tests/tokens.mjs` checks the colour system on Vici's values (`vici/dist/tokens.json`) in the light and the dark theme. It is the first suite in `node tests/run-all.mjs`.

**Contrast (WCAG 2.2).** Every text-on-fill and mark-on-surface pairing the app uses, the shared shell's and Repack Pilot's own (`tests/lib/prototype.mjs`: the cell board, the world model and the stage, What it is worth, the KPI board, the option cards and cost bars, the night console's marks, the intro's steps), in both themes: 4.5:1 for text (1.4.3), 3:1 for marks, icons, control borders and the focus ring (1.4.11). The test fails below that. It also fails when the app's CSS or JavaScript names a Vici token that does not exist, or when a pairing names a token one theme lacks.

**Colour-blind separation.** The night's state colours, goods at rest, readings, the cost bars, What it is worth's two bars, and merlot against danger, simulated for normal vision, protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia with the Machado et al. (2009) matrices at full severity in linear RGB. The former palette was generated to keep every pair at least 0.10 apart in OKLab; Vici's tones fall short of that in places, for example late and on time (danger and success: 0.030 in the light theme, 0.020 in the dark one), the cost bars' late store orders against doing nothing (warning and danger: 0.069 and 0.048), the two bars of What it is worth (0.061 and 0.023), a person's merlot frame against a reading's fjord in the dark theme (0.055), and merlot text and danger (a hue gap of 16.7° and a lightness gap of 0.03, where the former palette kept 18° and 0.12). The test reports these gaps rather than failing on them, because they are Vici's to fix: the app makes up for each in form (§3): the lanes' words, the bars' labels, the person's frame and icon.

**Contrast in the running page.** The token check tests pairs; `tests/smoke.mjs` (cases `contrast` and `contrast-customer`) measures every visible text element of the running page against its real background, at 1920×1080 and 1280×720, in the light and the dark theme: seventeen states of the Full view (the intro, the idle workspace, Diagnostics, the five drawer tabs, the mode choice, the key dialog with its message, a banner of each level, the scripted replay running, the replay with the drawer open, the three options and doing nothing, the drafts with the console's notice, the night's end with the summary card and the run facts, the management summary) and five of the Customer view in scenario 3. The measure (`tests/lib/contrast.mjs`) counts the fill Vici paints behind a tag's, a badge's or a marker's text as that text's background, and measures only the dialog while one is open. `tests/live.mjs` measures the end of a live run as well. Normal text needs 4.5:1 and large text 3:1; inactive controls have no duty (WCAG 1.4.3). Text still fading in is measured in a later state, at full opacity (`TECH_NOTES.md` D44). Each case plants a control first, text in Vici's strong border grey on the page's background, which falls short (3.59:1 light, 4.49:1 dark): the measure must catch it at exactly that ratio, or the case fails.

## 6. Agentic patterns

Yocco's six patterns for agentic AI, and where each one lives:

| Pattern | In Repack Pilot |
|---|---|
| Intent preview | The **progress rail** shows the agent's plan in five steps before a run starts (Perceive, Plan, Pick, Replan, Prepare). The intro window explains the world model instead (Knows, Checks, Learns, Asks), and the Customer view's flow shows each item's way before and during the night |
| Autonomy dial | A group of toggle buttons in the control bar (`js/controls.js`), drawn like the night speed: Advise, Approve (default) and Bounded, the chosen level in ink as the night speed's is; locked while a run goes on, because a run keeps the level it started with. Bounded carries a lock and stays locked until the shadow-mode gate is met: its tooltip names the criteria it fails, and a click opens the gate's dialog with every criterion against the synthetic record (`js/autonomy_view.js`) instead of switching. In Advise an option card's Approve reads **Apply**: the person's click applies it (`TECH_NOTES.md` D58) |
| Explainable rationale | The world-model panel's expected against observed, the corrections log, the agent's narration with its labels (*Check*, *Confirmed*, *Hypothesis*, *Revised: old → new*, *Finding*, *Recommendation*), the reasoning summaries, the AI label's explanation, and **Guardrail** lines when the code refuses a step |
| Confidence signal | Camera and grasp confidence on each detection; each option card's first line, what happens to the trucks "in all 20 nights" or "in n of 20 nights"; a provisional item-master entry marked *unverified* until a person confirms it (scenario 3) |
| Action audit and undo | The Audit tab and its JSON export. Undo is not needed: nothing leaves the outbox |
| Escalation pathway | Rules in code hand an item or a decision to a person with a one-line reason: an unknown barcode, a fragile item with low grasp confidence, falling grasp success, a store close to its cut-off; the agent explains, it cannot raise or lift an escalation |

**Dialogs** (`js/modal.js`, Vici's dialog on a native `<dialog>`) repeat "You are interacting with an AI system" in their footer, as Vici's lilac tag with the sparkle, since their backdrop blurs the header's (`TECH_NOTES.md` D69). The How it works window closes up on windows up to 900 px high (`css/modals.css`), so *What is real* stays in view without scrolling from 1280 × 720 to 1920 × 1080.

**Progress rail** (`js/progress.js`, on Vici's stepper). Five steps, the same as the plan, in one line above the console. Ink while the agent works, with a pulsing hexagon on the current step. Merlot with *Your decision*, *Your approval* or *Your confirmation* when the run waits for a person. A tick in ink when done, a stop mark where a run ended early, a dashed hexagon for steps a run did not need. It never moves backwards when the agent revisits an earlier phase. It is an ordered list, the current step carries `aria-current="step"`, and each step's state is spelled out for screen readers. On a window under 820 px high the Customer view drops the rail; the stage's moment says where the night is. The intro numbers the world model's four steps on Vici's stepper, each hexagon in the colour the stage uses for it: ink on Vici's neutral for what the model knows, fjord for the camera's and the scale's checks, the neutral solid (the model's own frame) for what it learns, merlot's soft tint for the person it asks; there is no state colour among the four.

**Handover** (`js/handover.js`). The run state *awaiting approval* covers three moments, so the status tag, the console's phase line and the rail all name the human step the same way: **Awaiting your decision** while the options wait, **Awaiting your approval** while the drafts wait, and **Awaiting your confirmation** while an escalated item waits. The tag says it waits for you only while something waits on screen; after you reject an option, it is the agent's turn again until it waits for your next decision (`TECH_NOTES.md` D42, tested).

## 6a. Decisions on screen

The decision moment is the demo's climax, so it follows Rebate Hunter's pattern closely (`js/options_panel.js`, `js/outbox.js`, `css/drawer.css`, `css/console.css`).

- **The Options tab** opens by itself when the options arrive, and the console's decision line has a **Review options** button. A summary column (*Decide how to respond*, the AI label, the comparison bars) stands beside four of Vici's cards: options A, B and C and **Do nothing**. Each card: its letter in a hexagon cell and its title with the *Recommended* tag on its frame, then **what happens to the trucks** over the 20 simulated nights, in the state's tone with Vici's icon for the state and its words (success *Every truck on time in all 20 nights*; warning when an order is late in some of them; danger when one is late in all, as doing nothing's *Soest late in 20 of 20 nights, Hamm in 17*), a line on what it does, **Extra labour** (a saving below zero), **Late orders, expected** as their cost (the count in the tooltip), the **expected total** in large figures, and the residual risk. The recommended card adds **Why recommended:** with the agent's reason when it is not the cheapest (`TECH_NOTES.md` D51). Every figure on a card comes from the engine's costing by variant id, never from the agent's text; the summary column says so. The comparison bars split each option's total into extra labour (fjord, the money an option spends) and late store orders (warning); doing nothing is one danger bar.
- **One emphasised action.** The recommended option has the merlot frame, the *Recommended* tag and the one solid merlot **Approve**; the other options keep secondary Approve buttons, and Reject is a neutral outline. Doing nothing is a dashed card with its own button. A decided card says *Approved*, *Rejected* or *Not selected* in a tag; the rest dim.
- **Fit.** Four columns on a 1080p projector, where the open drawer is a little taller (D54), two below a 1,000 px window height, and on a small laptop the cards drop their description so every Approve button stays in view (`TECH_NOTES.md` D43; `tests/layout.mjs`, the options state at five sizes).
- **The Outbox.** A line says it plainly: drafts only, no email, staffing or messaging connection; approving marks a draft cleared for a person to send. Each draft is a card with its kind in a neutral hexagon cell (call-in message, email, memo), recipient by function, subject, the body (folded after six lines), its status and the AI label; **Approve** in merlot and Reject beside it; *Approve all* when more than one waits. A draft with a figure the audit could not match carries an *Unchecked figures* tag.
- **The management summary** closes the run: a console card with the AI label and **Open management summary**, which opens the summary with *Copy text* and *Download .md*. A scripted replay's summary says so in its last line and its file name, and the download adds a SIMULATION line on top.
- **While you decide**, the night stands still: the board's clock shows its pause, and the clock tile says *paused: your decision*, *paused: your approval* or *paused: your confirmation*, otherwise *paused: the agent's turn*.

## 7. The cell board

The board is drawn from the night's events only (`js/cell.js`, `js/cell_data.js`): the night player emits them, the agent's tools and the scripted replays add the decisions and safety actions, and the board never asks which producer runs. A decision's effect shows at once, while the night still stands (`TECH_NOTES.md` D37). Its canvas is Vici's sunken background; its cards, stations and lanes are on the surface.

- **Frame.** A floating caption at top left (*Ruhrkorb DC Unna* on one line, the night's date where a scenario has one, the scenario); the night clock and the **Simulated cell** tag at top right, in both modes. At a wake-up the clock adds "· paused". In the short and strip sizes the head is one row: the caption keeps its first line, whole, a pause mark (two bars) stands in for the words, and the world-model chip's text gives way first.
- **The cell line.** Five stations from left to right, each with its icon in a hexagon cell and an arrow to the next over the gap between them, level with the icons (the arrows take no width, so the stations keep the room Inter's lines need): the inbound pallet (the next wave, units still to repack), the camera (the last item and its confidence; warning for a barcode the item master does not know), the robot cell (its success rate, over the last 100 grasps once there are 100, then the grip in use; a success or danger pulse on a sample of grasps; danger when E3 fires), the scale (the reading against the world model's weight, fjord when they differ by more than the tolerance) and the manual station (its queue and picks; merlot words for the units handed to a person). The camera's and the scale's cells are fjord, the readers; the others neutral. Each station's share of the row follows its longest lines (the camera names an item, the manual station two counts, the scale a weight); a compact board, without the second lines, gives the scale less and keeps every station's name whole down to 1280 px, with a wider gap so an arrow stays clear of the names (`TECH_NOTES.md` D80).

## 8. Built for the room

- **Projection.** Muted colours blend on a projector (VUMC). Pastels therefore only ever fill; the meaning sits in a deeper stroke or text step at 3:1 or more. The decision view is reviewed under a simulated washout (`contrast(0.62) brightness(1.18) saturate(0.8)`) and under protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia, in both themes.
- **Size.** The root font scales with the window, from 15 px up to about 17 px at 1920 px wide; **Larger text** enlarges the console. Vici's buttons, tabs, tags, menu items and fields keep the sizes the app had before Vici (§11). Every HTML control is at least 40 × 40 px and every board button 44 × 44 px, well above the 24 px minimum of WCAG 2.5.8 (`tests/layout.mjs`, five sizes in eight states of scenario 1 in the Full view and seven states of scenario 3 in the Customer view).
- **Focus.** Vici's focus ring: 2 px of `--vc-focus` with a 2 px gap, an outline on rectangles and a hexagonal ring on hexagon controls. Dialogs trap the focus and give it back when they close.
- **Emphasis.** One emphasised action per moment: *Start agent* when idle, the recommended option's Approve when a decision waits, Confirm entry when the world model asks.
- **Words.** Labels name what a person understands: *Start agent*, *Night speed*, *Your decision*, *Forget key*. The same action keeps the same name through the flow. The words on screen are a customer's: *simulated nights*, *a rule in code*, *handed to a person*, *agent turns*, item and store names (the strip's store chips use three-letter store codes); tool names, rule codes, SKU ids and seeds stay in Details, the popovers and the audit, and the script checker refuses them in a script's words (`TECH_NOTES.md` D55). The header keeps its height with Inter's wider text: "How it works" sits beside the mode badge, the tiles' second lines wrap at most once, and the figures step down as §7a says. The AI disclosure keeps its full sentence, "You are interacting with an AI system", at 1280 px and wider.
- **Motion.** A spinner while something loads, dialogs and console entries fading in, the rail's pulse during a run, the cell's grasp pulse and a new lane sliding in. All of it stops under `prefers-reduced-motion` (`tests/smoke.mjs`, case `board`).

## 10. Changing things safely

**Keep the sister apps in step.** Repack Pilot, Rebate Hunter, the Navigator and the Timetable Translator share one shell: `vici/` (the same Vici release, unchanged), the five shared stylesheets (`css/style.css`, `css/components.css`, `css/console.css`, `css/drawer.css`, `css/modals.css`), each Rebate Hunter's file down to its app-specific marker with Repack Pilot's own rules in the block below it, `js/theme.js` and the shell modules (`js/dom.js`, `js/md.js`, `js/modal.js`, `js/progress.js`, `js/drawer.js`), and four tests that are the same file in every prototype: `tests/tokens.mjs`, `tests/hexagon.mjs`, `tests/page-icon.mjs` and `tests/vici-files.mjs`. What differs between the prototypes in those tests (the colour pairs only one prototype draws, the tour the hexagon test takes, the page icon) lives in one place, `tests/lib/prototype.mjs`. The cell board's, the world model's, the stage's and the KPI board's stylesheets (`css/cell.css`, `css/world.css`, `css/stage.css`, `css/kpiboard.css`) are Repack Pilot's own. Change a shared file in all four repositories in the same way, or the prototypes drift apart.

**Colours come from Vici.** There is no palette to regenerate any more. To use a colour, pick the semantic token whose meaning fits (§3, §4), never a family primitive or a value of your own, and add the pairing to the contrast test: to `SHELL_PAIRS` in `tests/tokens.mjs` for the shared shell, to `COLOUR_PAIRS` in `tests/lib/prototype.mjs` for Repack Pilot's own parts. To change a colour, change Vici: a fix belongs in Vici's next release, which then comes here as described in `vici/README.md` (copy the files, change the version, run the tests, look at the prototype in light and dark). Never edit the copy in `vici/`. `favicon.svg` is the one file with colour values of its own: change it together with the website's card.

**Keep the grammar.**
- Merlot only for what a person does or decides, and never as a mark among state colours: a person shows in words, the person's icon and the merlot frame.
- State colours only for the night's states, and always with a word, an icon or a shape.
- Lilac only for what the AI wrote or thinks. New AI-written content gets an AI label.
- The tape only for scripted replays; the cell's own tag stays neutral.
- No colour values in components: use a token. Anything round is a hexagon; anything pill-shaped a hex capsule.
- New interactive elements are at least 40 × 40 px (44 px on the board) and use Vici's focus ring, hexagonal on a hexagon.
