      How the showcase works

      A robot cell repacks 3,000 units for 12 stores overnight

      One night shift in a grocery DC: an AI agent plans it, a robot cell repacks mixed pallets into store orders, and people take the rest. For heads of DC operations asking what a cell is worth.

      Tonight at Ruhrkorb DC Unna, fictional
      Units to repack3,000
      Store orders12, first truck at 01:35
      For the robot2,100 units
      For people900 units it may not grip

      The robot works from a model of every item

      The world model holds each item's weight, size, fragility and grip. The agent plans the night with it, and camera and scale check every pick against it.

      The world model at work
      KnowsEvery item on the pallet, before the night starts.
      ChecksCamera and scale compare each item with the model; the robot rehearses every grasp first.
      LearnsA reading that clearly differs is written back for every unit of that item.
      AsksWhat the model doesn't know goes to a person, who decides.

      The cell is simulated, the arithmetic is real

      Ruhrkorb, its stores and its robot cell are invented. The agent's words are a scripted replay of its run, and an engine computes every hour and euro as the night plays in your browser.

      Where the figures come from
      SimulatedThe robot cell, its camera and scale, the warehouse system, the retailer and its stores.
      Sourced400 picks an hour by hand (Fraunhofer IML) and €35.80 an hour of labour (Eurostat).
      AssumedThe robot may take 70 % of units, grasps 91 % of them and tries 500 an hour.

      €342,069 a year for a DC of 6,000,000 units

      The engine prices the same work by hand and with the cell. The yearly figure is gross, before cell costs: a cell's cost differs by site, so there's no payback claim.
